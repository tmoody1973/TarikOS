TARIK OS: FEATURE LIST
Last updated 2026-08-13

A personal AI assistant named Zola. You reach her by voice on a page, by text,
or by calling a real phone number. 49 tools. Open source, MIT.
github.com/tmoody1973/TarikOS


THE SPINE

1. TELOS
   A structured record of what you are actually working toward: mission, goals
   (each with a measurable finish line), problems, challenges, strategies. She
   interviews you for it once, then maintains it. A summary rides into every
   conversation, so she connects today's errand to this year's goal without
   being asked. It updates itself nightly from your conversations and journal,
   linking every change back to where it came from. Concept from Daniel
   Miessler's TELOS framework.

2. THE MORNING BRIEF
   One page assembled before you wake: your day, the email that matters, the
   headlines worth your time, and where your goals stand. Opens with GOALS, not
   your inbox. Say "run my brief" to build a fresh one on the spot.

3. THE LEDE
   Every brief now opens with a paragraph written by a model that read the whole
   brief plus the previous one. It connects things the sections keep apart, a
   meeting that collides with another, an email that touches a goal, a headline
   worth your time. Zola reads that paragraph and stops; the sections are there
   if you ask. Same paragraph on the page, in the Telegram digest, and out loud.

4. WEEKLY REVIEW
   A review brief builds Sunday morning. Zola walks it with you item by item,
   asking whether each goal still stands, changed, or is done, and records your
   answers.


THE WORKSPACE

5. MAIL CENTER (/mail)
   Read full Gmail threads in the app across multiple accounts, compose and
   reply with rich text. Say "draft a reply to the X email saying..." and she
   writes it with the thread in front of her; it lands as a badged draft in your
   real Gmail. There is no send function anywhere in her tool surface.

6. ZOLA'S OWN INBOX (/mail/zola)
   She has her own address, zola@tarikos.app. Services write to her, you forward
   her things, strangers reach an agent rather than you. The rule is one
   sentence: she writes to you freely, and drafts to everyone else. A stranger
   who writes in gets one automatic letter explaining the containment.

7. STUDIO (/studio)
   A real writing workspace. Five document types (note, draft, brief, plan,
   decision record), each from its own template. Tables, lists, media, DOCX
   export, version snapshots you can restore, and references pointing a document
   at whatever it grew out of. Ask her to tighten a paragraph and she quotes your
   own words back to find it, then leaves a suggestion waiting for you to take
   or leave. She never edits your writing.

8. PROJECTS (/projects)
   A board backed by Plane, so you never open Plane. "Add calling the bank to my
   list" is a task. "Start a project for the pledge drive with these five tasks"
   gets read back to you before anything is created. The board reads Plane live
   and keeps no copy, because a copy is a thing that can disagree.

9. CONTACTS (/contacts)
   4,370 Google contacts, synced nightly, searchable by name, number or email.
   She can add, update and delete, writing straight through to Google. Two
   matches is a question, never a guess. An edit reports what it displaced,
   because Google replaces a whole field at a time.

10. DOCUMENTS (/documents)
    Turn a brief, a research result or a Studio document into a real file and
    hand it to someone. Share links are minted per visit with an expiry, a
    download cap, and a revoke button. Sharing takes a spoken confirmation;
    saving a file for yourself does not.

11. HABITS (/habits)
    Identity-based pillars, not a checklist. Completion is graded (minimum,
    standard, beyond, intentionally skipped) because "I did the two-minute
    version" and "I did the full thing" are different truths. There is no streak
    counter anywhere. The number shown is how often you came back after a gap.
    An intentional skip carries no penalty. The evening check-in waits on the
    dashboard and has no way to notify you, so it cannot nag.

12. CALENDAR BY VOICE
    List, create and move events, with a read-back before every write.

13. REMINDERS
    Due dates and nudges that reach you by Telegram or email, wherever you are.


HOW YOU REACH HER

14. VOICE, ON THE PAGE
    Press ENGAGE, or arm the wake word and just say her name.

15. WAKE WORD: "HEY ZOLA"
    A 161 KB model trained from scratch on a rented GPU for under a dollar.
    Runs in the browser, no account and no key. Open source, Apache-2.0.

16. SHE HANGS UP
    Say goodbye and the call ends because the conversation ended, not because
    you pressed something.

17. PHONE
    A real number. Call it and she answers with the same brain and the same
    tools. No app, no browser, no session to start. She can call you too.

18. TELEGRAM
    The same assistant over text with a deliberately narrower tool surface.
    Anything whose safety rests on a spoken confirmation stays on voice, and
    every exclusion is written down with its reason.

19. VIEWPORT
    A slide-in panel showing a real hosted browser she drives by voice while you
    watch. Click into the frame to take over. Findings become a brief with a
    session-replay link.

20. VOICE CONSOLE
    Microphone waveform, live transcripts, and a tool-activity matrix, so you
    can watch her work.


RUNNING IN THE BACKGROUND

21. SEMANTIC MEMORY
    Conversations consolidated nightly and recalled by meaning, not keywords.

22. VOICE JOURNALING
    Talk your day into it. A nightly pass mines entries for goal signals.

23. RESEARCH WORKFLOWS
    Scheduled or on-demand multi-step research (RSS plus web search), delivered
    as briefing cards with an in-app reader.

24. FEED MANAGER
    Add news sources by voice or by pasting a URL. Feeds are autodiscovered and
    validated before saving, with a health dot on each.


HOW ANYONE KNOWS IT WORKS

25. CONTROL PANEL (/control)
    Every tool's health, its last error, and an enable/disable switch. A tool
    turned off is blocked at the route before it can act, not merely hidden.

26. OBSERVABILITY
    Every tool call emits a span with its real outcome (success, no_match,
    ambiguous, disabled, error), kept deliberately separate from the ok flag
    Zola speaks from. Several tools answer "nothing matched" as a helpful
    sentence; counting those as successes would quietly corrupt every number
    built on the data. Full conversations arrive as traces with tool calls
    attached to the turn that produced them.

27. TOOL-SELECTION EVALS
    Real past utterances become a labelled dataset. The harness replays them
    against the live tool definitions and scores which tool the model reaches
    for. Two identical runs disagree on about 9% of utterances, so it reports
    that noise floor rather than letting anyone read meaning into a small move.

28. 980 TESTS
    Including guardrails that assert what the code is wired to, not just what it
    returns: that no tool can send email, that the lede writer holds no tools,
    that a disabled tool is blocked at the route.


THE RULE UNDERNEATH ALL OF IT

She can do a great deal on her own, and almost nothing on her own authority.
She drafts, proposes and prepares. You release.
